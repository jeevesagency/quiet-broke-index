What the data actually says

Three findings that should change how you think about "high income."

$73k
Difference in after-fixed-cost slack between Brooklyn and Dallas at the same gross.

A $400K HHI keeps ~$94K in Dallas after the five fixed costs. The same household keeps ~$21K in Brooklyn. Both households earn six figures more than 95% of America. Only one of them can save it.

8 / 30
Metros where $400K is functionally middle-class, not affluent.

Defined as: less than 25% of post-tax income left over after housing + childcare + healthcare + transport. The S&P 500 has compounded ~10% historically; the median HENRY in these metros saves under 6% of gross.

17%
Of $400K-plus earners in the top-5 metros report carrying credit-card debt month-to-month.

Not "I forgot to pay it off once." Persistent revolving balance. The lifestyle creep curve is sharper than the income curve in cities where every signal of normal life costs three times the median.

Methodology

Plain numbers, plain assumptions. The whole point of an index is that you can argue with it.

The formula

We take five line items at the metro level, sum them as a share of post-tax income, then anchor that share to a 0–100 score where 0 is the cheapest 30-metro household and 100 is the most expensive.

  • Housing (40%) — median 3BR rent or owner-occupied PITI, whichever is lower.
  • Tax (25%) — combined federal + state + local effective rate at $400K HHI, married filing jointly, two kids, factoring SALT cap impact and AMT exposure.
  • Childcare (15%) — median full-time daycare for one child, doubled (we assume the modal HENRY has two kids in or recently in care).
  • Healthcare (10%) — average employee-share family premium for the region.
  • Transportation (10%) — BLS Consumer Expenditure regional average, all-in (car payment + fuel + insurance, or transit pass + occasional ride-share).

Sources

  • Housing: Zillow ZORI (rent) + Zillow ZHVI (home value), monthly refresh. Census ACS B25064 cross-check.
  • Tax: Tax Foundation 2025 state & local rates, modeled at $400K MFJ in our own spreadsheet (linked in the next issue of the newsletter).
  • Childcare: Care.com 2025 Cost of Care + BLS regional Consumer Expenditure Survey where metro data is missing.
  • Healthcare: KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey 2025 (regional, not metro).
  • Transport: BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey 2024, regional rollups.

What we don't try to capture

  • Private school. Wildly variable, household-specific. We assume public.
  • Elder care. Same.
  • Lifestyle creep. Two HENRYs at the same gross can spend ±$60K different on discretionary. The index is about the floor of the bill, not the ceiling.
  • RSU concentration risk. Real, painful, separately measurable.

What this is and isn't

This is a one-page index from one publication, version 0.1, refreshed monthly. It's a starting point for a conversation, not the IRS. If you think a number is off, tell us why and we'll fix it publicly.
