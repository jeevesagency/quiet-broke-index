Cleveland Quiet-Broke Index: How squeezed is a $400K household in Cleveland?

Cleveland earns a Quiet-Broke score of 24 out of 100, ranking it 27 of 30 metros tracked in this index — the higher the score, the more squeezed a $400K household feels. Cleveland is one of the most slack-rich metros in the index. A $400K household keeps roughly $188,360 a year after taxes and the four major fixed costs — closer to $188k of breathing room than most of America's coasts can dream of. The thing to be honest about: comfort here is mostly geography, not virtue. The same gross in San Francisco or New York would feel completely different. For the full methodology behind these numbers, see the methodology page and the original Henry Finance deep dive on the Quiet-Broke Index.

What drives Cleveland's score

Five line items go into the index. Here is how Cleveland stacks up on each, relative to the 30-metro median:

Housing

Housing is one of Cleveland's genuine advantages. The median 3BR clocks in at $1,550/month, just 4.7% of a $400K gross — far below the 30-metro median of $2,750 (8.3% of gross). That gap alone is worth $14,400 a year in slack.

Taxes

Taxes are near the 30-metro median. The combined effective rate for MFJ at $400K is 38.3% (median: 38.3%), which translates to $153,200 in annual taxes. No particular advantage or disadvantage here.

Childcare

Childcare is a relative relief. At $1,120/month per child, two kids in full-time care runs $26,880/year — $11,040 less than the 30-metro median. That's real savings for the family-formation cohort.

Healthcare

Health premiums (employee share) are close to the 30-metro average at $670/month, or $8,040/year. Not a distinguishing factor in Cleveland's score.

Transport

Transport costs are lower than most metros at $410/month all-in. At $4,920/year, this is one area where Cleveland provides a modest offset against higher costs elsewhere.

Who feels the squeeze most in Cleveland

Run the numbers for a $400K dual-income Cleveland household with two kids: taxes take $153,200/year. Housing at $18,600/year. Two kids in daycare at $1,120/month each: $26,880/year. Health and transport: $12,960/year. Fixed obligations total $211,640, leaving $188,360/year in post-fixed slack. For comparison, the same household in New York (score 66) keeps only $96,720/year — $91,640 less. That difference, compounded over a decade, is a material wealth gap.

What the data leaves out

The tax rate used here is an effective rate estimate for a $400K MFJ household in Cleveland — it blends federal, state, and city income tax with payroll taxes, and does not account for RSU timing, alternative minimum tax, or SALT deduction phase-outs, all of which can shift your actual bill by several percentage points.

The childcare figure covers full-time daycare. Private school tuition in Cleveland can add $15,000 to $40,000/year per child once kids hit school age — a scenario the index doesn't capture.

This model assumes a two-earner household. Single-income households at $400K face a structurally different tax profile (often more favorable at this income level due to bracket differences), and households where one partner has large RSU cliff vests will see income concentration risk that changes the picture significantly from year to year.
